Lawyer meeting · tomorrow

What we need answered to start the pilot

A football academy pilot with roughly 30 children starts in two weeks. This is not the full legal agenda — company formation, trademark and IP assignment can follow. These six questions are the ones that decide whether the pilot can lawfully begin on time.

The one framing question

What is the minimum lawful path to run this pilot?

There is no company yet, no terms, no privacy policy. The pilot is unpaid, one academy, eight weeks, about 30 children aged 13–18.

Ask first: can a pilot of this shape run on a pilot agreement plus parental consent, with formation and the full policy set following behind — or does something have to exist before a single child's data is processed? Everything below is a detail of that answer.

Six decisions

In priority order

Ordered so that if the meeting runs short, the unanswered ones are the ones that can wait.

01
Blocks everything else

Who is controller and who is processor — Trak or the academy?

The academy holds the relationship with the families, which points to academy-as-controller and Trak-as-processor. We want that confirmed rather than assumed.

Why it is first: the answer decides who collects the parental consents, who answers a parent's access request, and who is liable if something goes wrong. The consent wording cannot be drafted until it is settled.

02
Sits in the demo

A coach types a child's name and age before anyone has consented. Is that lawful?

The coach builds the squad by hand as the second step of the pilot — name, position, shirt number, age group — and the child may not sign up for days, or at all. Some never will.

Options if it is not lawful: consent must precede roster entry; or the academy's own existing consent covers it; or the roster holds something less identifying (initials, a code) until the child joins. The third would need a product change, so we need to know quickly.

03
Needed before week 0

Parental consent — what must it say, who collects it, and in what form?

Ages 13–18. We would like to know from what age a child can consent for themselves in Greece, whether written or digital consent is required, and whether the academy can collect it on our behalf.

We have a full inventory of what is stored and who can read it, ready to attach.

04
Needed before week 0

What must the pilot agreement and DPA contain, and who drafts them?

One academy, eight weeks, no payment either way. We would rather have a short correct document than a long one.

Also: the pilot ends with a promise to hand the academy their data and honour deletion requests. Account deletion is built; data export is not. We need to know whether a manual export satisfies portability for a pilot this size.

05
Sub-processors

US providers, and one that receives a child's data directly

Supabase (database, auth, storage), Lovable (hosting), Sentry (error monitoring). All US.

The one to look at closely: the player feedback screen sends a child's first name, position, six performance scores and the coach's written note to Google Gemini through Lovable's AI gateway, to generate advice. That is a child's data leaving the platform to a US AI provider. We need to know what has to be in place, or whether that feature should be off for the pilot.

06
Can follow

Personal liability until the company exists

Everything currently happens in a personal capacity. What is genuinely exposed while running a pilot with children's data and no entity — and is that a reason to accelerate formation, or is insurance or the pilot agreement enough for eight weeks?

Before the meeting

Two things worth doing tonight

1. Check whether wellness_logs is empty, and drop it if so. It stores mood, energy, sleep quality and free-text notes per child — plausibly special category data, which would raise the bar to explicit consent and probably a DPIA. Nothing writes to it; the feature was cut. Dropping it removes special category data from tomorrow's conversation entirely, which is likely to make the meeting shorter and the advice cheaper.

2. Bring the data inventory. It lists all twenty tables, what each holds about a child, and who can read it — read from the schema, not from memory. A lawyer can answer questions 02 to 05 far faster with it than without.

The ask

What we want to leave with

  • A yes or no on whether the pilot can start in two weeks, and if not, what specifically has to exist first.
  • A decision on controller versus processor, so consent drafting can begin immediately.
  • An answer on the pre-consent roster, because the alternative is a product change with a two-week runway.
  • Who drafts what, by when, and at what cost — consent wording, pilot agreement, DPA.
  • What can safely wait until after the pilot: formation, trademark, IP assignments, full terms and privacy policy.
