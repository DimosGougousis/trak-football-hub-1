# Trak Football — Business Model Matrix

*Framework: the five parts of a business (Josh Kaufman, The Personal MBA).*
*Purpose: force clarity on each part, per persona, and let the sales pitch fall out of it.*

---

## The honest starting point

Two of the five parts are **fully built**, one is **partly built**, and two **do not exist at all**.

| Part | State today |
|---|---|
| 1. Create value | Built and verified — the product works end to end |
| 2. Attract attention | Nothing. No marketing, no landing narrative, no channel |
| 3. Sales | Nothing. **No one has ever paid for Trak** |
| 4. Deliver value | Built for coach, parent and club. Thin for the athlete |
| 5. Bring in money | Nothing. No billing, no price, no decision on who pays |

Writing this down is the point of the exercise: Trak is a **working product without a business
around it**. That is a normal and fixable position, but it should not be mistaken for being close
to revenue.

---

## Part 1 — Create value

**Why** — Youth football runs on opinion. A coach's judgement of a child lives in their head, is never written down, and disappears when the coach or the season changes. Nobody can see whether a child is actually developing  
**What** — A trustworthy, continuous record of a young player's development — performance *and* conduct — that a coach produces in minutes and a parent, player and club can all see  
**How** — Coach logs matches and assesses six dimensions → a band. Player receives feedback and carries an Evolution Card and Passport. Parent observes. Club sees consistency across its coaches  
**The need** — Without this Trak is a diary. The value is not the data — it is that the data is **believable**  

> **The insight that should govern every decision:** the product is not the tracking. It is the
> **trust**. A parent believing a rating, an academy acting on it, a child taking it seriously.
> This is why the write-authorisation flaw mattered so much more than a normal bug: a player able
> to fabricate their own assessments did not break a feature, it destroyed the only thing being
> sold.

---

## Part 2 — Attract attention

**Why** — Nobody is looking for Trak. Coaches do not search for "player assessment software" — they are volunteers and part-timers with no budget line and no habit of buying tools  
**What** — Reach the **club or academy**, not the individual coach. One conversation covers 10 coaches and 200 families  
**How** — Not ads. Direct relationships: academies, then schools. The Greek market first, where founder relationships are real. The Evolution Card and Passport are inherently shareable — a proud parent posting their child's card is the cheapest marketing that exists  
**The need** — A single, repeatable sentence that makes a director of football lean forward. That sentence is the output of this whole document  

**Do not build a marketing funnel yet.** With zero paying customers the goal is not scale, it is
**one pilot that works and can be pointed at**.

---

## Part 3 — Turn interest into payment

**Why** — Everything Trak does costs money to run and nothing comes in. Without this part Trak is a hobby  
**What** — A pilot → paid conversion for an academy or school. Not self-serve signups  
**How** — One cohort, one term, free or near-free. Agree success measures *with* them up front. Convert on evidence, not enthusiasm  
**The need** — **The decision on who pays.** Until that is settled, nothing here can be built  

### Who pays — the central unresolved question

The people who get the most value for the least effort are **not** the people doing the work.

| Persona | Effort required | Value received | Can they pay? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coach | **High** — logs everything | High | Rarely. Often volunteers |
| Athlete | Low today | Medium | No |
| Parent | **None** | High | Yes, but pays per child — small and churny |
| Club / Academy | **None** | High | **Yes. Has a budget** |

**Recommendation: the club or academy pays.** One sale covers many coaches and hundreds of
families, they hold the budget, they carry the reputational motive, and it avoids charging a
volunteer coach for the privilege of doing unpaid work. Parent-pays looks tempting because parents
care most emotionally, but it monetises a child's development record directly to their family —
uncomfortable in itself, and worse for a school pilot.

## Part 4 — Deliver value

**Why** — Value promised and not felt is worse than never promising it. In youth sport a tool that gets abandoned mid-season damages the club's trust in you permanently  
**What** — Each persona must feel the value **in their own terms**, not ours  
**How** — Coach: fast, low-friction, makes them look professional. Athlete: feedback comes back and progress is visible. Parent: sees their child without having to ask. Club: consistency across coaches  
**The need** — Delivery is where Trak is currently strongest — and where the one real hole is  

### What we risk, and how we mitigate it

| Risk | Why it is real | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Coach stops logging** | They do all the work and are unpaid. If it takes more than a couple of minutes it stops in week three | Ruthless friction reduction. Quick assess, quick log. Treat every extra tap as a threat to the business |
| **Athlete never opens it** | Today the app is *done to* them — they receive, they never act | The character feature is the answer: the one place the child does the work |
| **Parent sees an empty screen** | Has happened twice already — finished screens returning nothing behind a missing permission | Never ship a persona view without exercising it as that persona against real data |
| **Ratings are not believed** | If the record can be gamed, or a coach rates carelessly, the whole proposition dies | Authorisation is enforced, not assumed. Assessments are immutable. The fairness flag exists |
| **Season ends and it dies** | Youth football is seasonal; attention drops in summer | The Passport is a *career* record. Value accumulates across seasons — the longer you use it the more it is worth leaving |

### How we measure delivered value

Not vanity metrics. For each persona, the one number that says value landed:

| Persona | The measure that matters |
|---|---|
| Coach | % of sessions and matches actually logged, in week 8 — not week 1 |
| Athlete | Do they open the app **between** matches? |
| Parent | Do they return without being prompted? |
| Club | Assessment consistency across coaches; does the director cite it in a real decision? |

---

## Part 5 — Bring in enough money to keep going

**Why** — Trak currently has running costs and no revenue. Company formation, accountant and contributions arrive before the first euro does  
**What** — Recurring revenue from clubs and schools. Per-squad or per-club, annual, aligned to the season  
**How** — Does not exist. There is no billing code, no plans, no prices  
**The need** — Price cannot be set from a spreadsheet. It comes from the pilot — what an academy says out loud when asked what this is worth

## The persona matrix — value, effort, emotion

This is the part that becomes the pitch. **Nobody buys features; they buy relief from a fear.**

| Persona | What they actually want | What they fear | The sentence that lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coach** | To be taken seriously as a developer of players, not a weekend volunteer | Looking disorganised. Being unable to justify a decision to an angry parent | "You will never again be asked why a child isn't playing and have nothing to show" |
| **Athlete** | To know whether they are good enough, and to be *seen* | Being overlooked. Working hard and nobody noticing | "Your season is recorded. What you did doesn't disappear when the coach changes" |
| **Parent** | To know their child is progressing and being cared for | Being in the dark. Their child quietly neglected while others are favoured | "You'll see how your child is doing without having to corner the coach after training" |
| **Club / Academy** | Reputation and consistency across coaches | A coach damaging the club's name. Losing families to a rival academy | "Every coach assesses to the same standard, and you can see it" |

---

## What Trak is — and what it is not

Being explicit about the "is not" is what keeps the product from drifting.

| Trak **is** | Trak **is not** |
|---|---|
| A development record — performance and character | A scouting or talent-ID marketplace |
| Coach-led and coach-owned | A place where children rate themselves |
| A long-term career passport | A match-day statistics product |
| A tool for the average academy | An elite-club analytics suite competing with Hudl or Veo |
| Honest about what it measures | A promise to make anyone a professional |
| Private between coach, child and parent | A public leaderboard or a comparison engine |

The last row is a commercial decision, not just an ethical one: the moment children are publicly
ranked, schools stop returning calls.

---

## The pitch this produces

> Youth football runs on opinion. A coach's judgement lives in their head, is never written down,
> and vanishes when the season or the coach changes — so nobody can see whether a child is
> actually developing.
>
> Trak gives every coach in your academy the same two-minute routine, and turns it into a record
> a parent believes, a player is proud of, and you can point to.
>
> Same standard across every coach. Nothing hidden from parents. Nothing that ranks one child
> against another.

---

## The one decision that unblocks the most

**Who pays.** Parts 3 and 5 cannot be built until it is settled, and it changes the product:
per-club billing needs organisation-level accounts and seats, per-parent billing needs consumer
payments and subscription management — very different work.

Recommendation as above: **the club or academy pays**, validated by the pilot rather than assumed.

## Open questions for discussion

1. Do we agree the club pays — or is there a case for the school as the buyer, given the
   character direction?
2. Is the athlete a *user* or a *beneficiary*? The character feature only makes sense if they are
   a user, and that changes what we promise.
3. What is the smallest thing an academy would pay for **today**, before the character feature?
4. Do we sell development, or do we sell *evidence*? They attract different buyers.
