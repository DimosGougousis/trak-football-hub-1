# Trak — Character Building Feature: Where we'd value your expertise

**Trak** is a football development app. Today the **coach** is the main user — they log sessions
and rate players — while the **young players are largely passive**. We want to add a
**character-building feature**: it teaches behavioural skills to the players, gives *them* an
active role, and shows the coach each player's **character progress alongside performance** —
"the complete player."

Given your work leading sports psychology at Panathinaikos across **both the youth setup and the
first team**, your practical, on-the-ground insight would be invaluable in shaping this properly —
more than any framework could give us.

## What we've drafted so far

- **Six values:** Composure · Respect · Effort · Teamwork · Responsibility · Sportsmanship
  (plus carrying them *beyond* football).
- **How it's delivered:** short, gamified moments triggered when the coach logs a training or
  match — **learn** (flashcards) → **apply** (a real football dilemma) → **act** (a real-world
  challenge to do at training). A weekly value focus; content deepens over the season.
- **Age-appropriate (our responsibility, not the coach's):** content auto-matched to the team's
  age, in three rough bands — ~6–9 (concrete, rule-based), ~10–13 (fairness and consequences),
  ~14–18 (abstract, identity, leadership).
- **No grading or ranking:** progress shows as streaks, badges, and a "character" lens on the
  player card — never a score, never one child compared to another.

## Where we'd value your help (exactly)

1. **The values** — from your academy experience, are these the right behaviours to build in
   young players, and which matter most at each age?
2. **What actually works** — how did you develop character and conduct with youth players in
   practice? What real methods should we be encoding, not guessing?
3. **Age-appropriateness** — are our age bands and their complexity right? What is realistic to
   expect a child to understand and do at each stage?
4. **Engagement vs. authenticity** — does gamifying character (badges, streaks) help, or risk
   kids "performing" for rewards instead of meaning it? How do you keep it genuine?
5. **Self-reflection** — is asking young players to reflect on their own behaviour useful and
   safe at these ages? How honest are they, and how should we handle that?
6. **Red lines** — anything here you'd avoid or change: content, tone, or what we claim it does.

## The ask

A conversation to pressure-test and reshape this with your experience — and whether you'd be open
to **reviewing the content** and advising as we build.
