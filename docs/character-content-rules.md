# Trak Character — Content Rulebook

*Rules for anyone writing scenarios, flashcards or reflection prompts.*
*Derived from the review by Maria Psychountaki, sports psychologist. Follow these without needing
the theory behind them — the reasoning is noted where it helps.*

---

## The four rules that override everything

**1. Never identify the child with the mistake.**
Address the action, never the person. *"That reaction cost the team"* — never *"you are hot-headed."*
Her words: handled this way a mistake becomes *"a lesson and not a trauma."* She confirmed there is
no psychological risk in scenarios about failure, conflict or losing **provided this rule holds**.

**2. Never grade, score or rank character.**
Confirmed as correct. Progress is shown as values demonstrated and season progress — never a number,
never one child against another.

**3. Do not increase screen time.**
The strongest warning in her review, raised twice unprompted. The programme must not *"reinforce
addiction to electronic media."* Design target: **a few minutes, 2–3 times a week, or once at the
end of the week. Never daily, never continuous.**

**4. Reflection must be guided, never open-ended.**
*"It is essential to have questions that substantially guide the child through their reflection."*
Never ship a blank box. Always ask something specific.

---

## Rhythm

- **One value per week** — confirmed as *"a very good rhythm."*
- Within that week: **many different scenarios and instructions** for applying the value.
- **Repetition across the season** is expected, not a failure of imagination.
- Engagement is capped by rule 3 above. If a design increases how often a child opens the app, it
  is wrong regardless of how well it performs.

## Volume — the hard requirement

**Multiple scenarios per value per age band. This is not optional and not about variety.**

Her answer on gaming the system: social desirability *"cannot be avoided"* in any self-report
process. The only mitigation is **many different scenarios for the same value**, so that a child who
exaggerates in one is caught by inconsistency across others.

So scenario volume does two jobs at once — teaching, and making self-report readable. Thin content
breaks both.

## Age bands

Confirmed as sound. *"It is important that the scenarios correspond to each level."*

| Band | Scenario style |
|---|---|
| **~6–9** | Concrete, rule- and authority-based. Short. One idea per scenario |
| **~10–13** | Fairness, intent and consequences. Choices with a defensible answer |
| **~14–18** | Abstract, identity, leadership, competing goods. No clean answer required |

**Watch for over-confidence in the youngest band.** *"Young children tend to overestimate their
abilities."* Their self-ratings should be treated as a conversation starter, not a measurement.

**Two anchors, and they are not interchangeable** — a correction from her review:
- **Kohlberg → moral development.** Use for how a child reasons about right and wrong.
- **Piaget → cognitive development.** Use for what a child can process at all.

Optionally frame content against the **developmental domains**: cognitive, physical, social,
personality.

## The values

Confirmed: **Composure · Respect · Effort · Teamwork · Responsibility · Sportsmanship**

Proposed additions, for a product decision — the last two are notably sport-specific and have no
current equivalent:

| Candidate | Note |
|---|---|
| Discipline | |
| Cooperation | Overlaps with Teamwork — probably merge rather than add |
| **Managing winning and losing** | No current equivalent. Strong candidate |
| **Resilience to criticism** | No current equivalent. Strong candidate |

## The "act" step

Validated: *"the 'do' is needed because it reinforces learning."*

**But it must carry proper instructions.** A real-world challenge cannot be a vague nudge — it needs
to say plainly what to do, when, and what counts as having done it.

## What we may claim

Her wording, and the ceiling on our claims:

> Children **get to know themselves better** and are **more ready to choose what is right**.

Not behaviour change. Not measurable character improvement. Not fewer incidents. Self-knowledge and
readiness — that is the honest claim and it came from the expert.

## Mandatory, non-negotiable

**Parental consent.** Her emphasis, in capitals: since every age band is minors, consent is
*"MANDATORY."* This is now a stated professional requirement for the lawyer conversation, not an
assumption of ours.

---

## Checklist before any scenario ships

- [ ] Addresses the action, never the child's character
- [ ] Matched to the age band's reasoning level
- [ ] Part of a set of several scenarios for that value, not a one-off
- [ ] Any reflection attached to it asks a specific, guiding question
- [ ] Any "act" step states exactly what to do and what counts as done
- [ ] Adds no pressure to open the app more often
- [ ] Contains no score, rank or comparison to another child
